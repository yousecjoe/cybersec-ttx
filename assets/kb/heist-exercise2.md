# Digital Vault: The Swiss Job
## A Heist-Themed Cybersecurity Tabletop Exercise

## Exercise Overview

**Title:** Digital Vault: The Swiss Job  
**Duration:** 4 hours (recommended)  
**Target Audience:** Security Officers, Intrusion Analysts, Digital Forensics, Bank Management  
**Difficulty:** Junior to Executive-level  
**Objective:** Test Zurich Global Bank's ability to detect, contain, and neutralize a sophisticated international team attempting to compromise the bank's digital infrastructure to steal both funds and confidential client information.

## Learning Objectives

1. Evaluate team coordination during complex security incidents targeting financial systems
2. Test response capabilities against a methodical, well-researched adversary
3. Assess communication protocols between technical security, compliance, and executives
4. Practice decision-making under pressure with regulatory and reputational considerations
5. Identify gaps in current security measures for international banking operations

## Exercise Structure

### Preparation Phase (2 weeks prior)

1. **Bank Director Selection:** Appoint 1-2 individuals to coordinate the exercise
2. **Bank Personnel Selection:** Identify key staff from various departments
3. **Resource Preparation:** Ready the necessary documentation, secure communication channels, and simulated banking environment
4. **Pre-Exercise Briefing:** Conduct a confidential briefing explaining exercise parameters and expectations

### Exercise Roles

1. **Bank Director:** Controls exercise flow, introduces scenarios, evaluates responses
2. **Security Officers:** Personnel responsible for monitoring banking systems
3. **Intrusion Analysts:** Specialists who investigate and respond to suspicious activities
4. **Digital Forensics:** Technical specialists who analyze compromised systems
5. **Bank Management:** Decision-makers who balance security with business operations
6. **Observers:** Record actions, decisions, and potential improvements
7. **Regulatory Representatives:** (Optional) Add realism with compliance considerations

## Scenario Background

Zurich Global Bank is a prestigious international financial institution catering to high-net-worth individuals and corporations. The bank's Security Division provides monitoring and protection for:

- Core Banking Platform (accounts, transfers, investments)
- Client Relationship Management System (client profiles and data)
- Interbank Transfer Network (SWIFT and international payments)
- Digital Banking Services (online and mobile banking)

The bank utilizes a sophisticated Security Operations Center with 24/7 monitoring, employing both automated systems and human analysts to protect its digital assets and maintain its century-old reputation for discretion and security.

## Exercise Narrative

An elite team of financial criminals known as "The Geneva Syndicate" has selected Zurich Global Bank as their target. The Syndicate combines expertise in banking operations, social engineering, and advanced cyber techniques. Their methodical approach involves months of research and preparation before executing precisely planned heists. Their objective at Zurich Global is twofold: compromising the bank's fund transfer systems for immediate financial gain while exfiltrating confidential client information for long-term exploitation. The attack will unfold in multiple stages over the course of the exercise, demonstrating a sophisticated understanding of banking operations and security protocols.

## Exercise Timeline and Scenarios

### Phase 1: Initial Detection (0:00-1:00)

**Setting the Scene (0:00-0:10)**
- Bank Director introduces the scenario as a normal business day
- Teams are performing routine security monitoring and client services

**Scenario 1 (0:10): Suspicious Email Communications**
- Security monitoring flags unusual email correspondence from a senior relationship manager
- The emails discuss client portfolio details with an external address
- The communication style matches the employee but the topics breach protocol

*Expected Actions:*
- Investigate the email communications
- Verify whether the relationship manager authorized the communications
- Review email metadata and recipient information
- Begin documenting the potential security incident

**Scenario 2 (0:30): Client Portal Anomalies**
- Monitoring systems detect unusual login patterns to the client portal
- Multiple high-value client accounts accessed during non-typical hours
- Each session remains within normal usage parameters but collectively forms a pattern

*Expected Actions:*
- Escalate the incident to senior security personnel
- Analyze the specific client accounts being targeted
- Begin tracing the origin of the suspicious logins
- Start assembling a specialized response team

**Scenario 3 (0:45): Security Bypass Attempt**
- Security systems detect attempts to bypass multi-factor authentication
- The attempts use legitimate client credentials but with systematic MFA manipulation
- Pattern suggests sophisticated understanding of the bank's security protocols

*Expected Actions:*
- Activate formal security incident procedures
- Implement additional verification for affected accounts
- Begin deeper investigation into authentication systems
- Consider enhanced monitoring of authentication attempts

### Phase 2: Escalation (1:00-2:00)

**Scenario 4 (1:00): Private Banker Compromise**
- A private banker reports receiving unusual text messages about clients
- Investigation reveals their corporate mobile device has been compromised
- The device contains sensitive client meeting notes and contact information

*Expected Actions:*
- Acknowledge connection to previously identified suspicious activities
- Isolate the compromised device for forensic analysis
- Determine what client information may have been exposed
- Update incident documentation and escalate to management

**Scenario 5 (1:20): Malware Discovery**
- Deep system analysis discovers sophisticated malware in the payments processing system
- The malware is designed to manipulate transfer amounts and destinations
- Evidence suggests it was planted weeks ago and has been dormant

*Expected Actions:*
- Perform detailed malware analysis
- Begin investigating initial infection vector
- Consider isolation of payment processing systems
- Update response team and executive management

**Scenario 6 (1:40): Data Exfiltration Detection**
- Network monitoring detects encrypted data transfers to unknown destinations
- The transfers coincide with client database access
- The exfiltration method uses legitimate backup channels to avoid detection

*Expected Actions:*
- Implement blocks on suspicious outbound connections
- Preserve evidence of the data breach
- Begin determining scope of potentially compromised client data
- Update risk assessment based on regulatory and reputational impact

### Phase 3: Crisis Management (2:00-3:00)

**Scenario 7 (2:00): Payment Anomalies**
- The international payments department reports unusual transfer patterns
- Several high-value transfers have been initiated with proper authorization but to suspicious destinations
- The transfers use a sophisticated chain of correspondent banks to obscure the final destination

*Expected Actions:*
- Prioritize freezing suspicious transactions
- Implement emergency verification for all high-value transfers
- Deploy specialists to trace the transaction paths
- Prepare for communication with correspondent banks

**Scenario 8 (2:20): Executive and Regulatory Involvement**
- The Bank President demands immediate briefing on the situation
- The Financial Regulatory Authority has been notified of potential issues
- Legal team requests assessment of disclosure requirements

*Expected Actions:*
- Prepare concise executive summary with confirmed facts
- Develop initial regulatory communication strategy
- Advise on client disclosure requirements and timing
- Continue technical response while addressing compliance concerns

**Scenario 9 (2:40): Syndicate Pattern Recognition**
- Security analysis identifies patterns matching known Geneva Syndicate activities
- Intelligence suggests immediate large-scale theft attempt is imminent
- Evidence points to specific systems as the likely targets

*Expected Actions:*
- Document complete threat intelligence
- Implement targeted hardening of identified systems
- Deploy all available security resources to monitor critical infrastructure
- Prepare for coordinated defense against sophisticated attack
- Consider contacting financial crime authorities

### Phase 4: Resolution and Recovery (3:00-4:00)

**Scenario 10 (3:00): Attack Methodology Identified**
- Forensic analysis reveals the complete attack methodology
- Evidence confirms Syndicate involvement with identified team members
- Analysis pinpoints vulnerabilities exploited and attack timeline

*Expected Actions:*
- Document comprehensive findings for leadership and law enforcement
- Develop a tailored defense strategy against identified techniques
- Prioritize critical vulnerability remediation
- Prepare detailed briefing on the attack methodology

**Scenario 11 (3:20): Containment Decision Point**
- The response team must decide on final containment strategy
- Options include complete system reset vs. surgical removal of compromised elements
- Each option has different impacts on banking operations and client services

*Expected Actions:*
- Evaluate business impact of each option
- Make decisions based on comprehensive risk assessment
- Communicate decisions and rationale to all stakeholders
- Begin implementing the chosen strategy

**Scenario 12 (3:40): Recovery Planning**
- With immediate threats contained, focus shifts to system restoration and client management
- Multiple banking systems require secure rebuilding
- Client communications strategy needs finalization
- Regulatory reporting requirements must be addressed

*Expected Actions:*
- Develop a prioritized recovery sequence
- Create a communication plan for different stakeholders
- Prepare regulatory compliance documentation
- Begin documenting lessons learned for security enhancements

### Conclusion (3:50-4:00)

- Bank Director declares the end of the exercise
- Brief initial feedback from participants
- Schedule a formal debrief session for the following day

## Exercise Evaluation

### Evaluation Metrics

1. **Detection Effectiveness**
   - Time to detect initial suspicious activities
   - Ability to correlate related security events
   - Thoroughness of investigation

2. **Response Efficiency**
   - Time from detection to initial response
   - Appropriateness of response actions
   - Resource allocation and deployment decisions

3. **Communication Effectiveness**
   - Internal security team communication
   - Management and regulatory updates
   - Client communication strategy development

4. **Decision Quality**
   - Financial loss prevention prioritization
   - Decision-making balancing security, operations, and compliance
   - Client confidentiality and trust considerations

### Post-Exercise Activities

1. **Initial Debrief (Immediately following exercise)**
   - Quick round-table discussion of initial impressions
   - Identification of major strengths and challenges
   - Collection of immediate feedback

2. **Formal Review (1-2 days after exercise)**
   - Structured review of exercise timeline and decisions
   - Analysis of major decision points
   - Documentation of lessons learned

3. **Improvement Planning (1-2 weeks after exercise)**
   - Development of specific action items
   - Assignment of responsibilities for improvements
   - Timeline for implementing changes

4. **Follow-up Exercise (3-6 months later)**
   - Targeted scenario to test improvements
   - Focus on previously identified weaknesses
   - Validate effectiveness of changes

## Bank Director Guidelines

### Pre-Exercise Preparation

1. **Scenario Customization**
   - Adjust technical details to match your institution's specific systems
   - Modify syndicate capabilities as appropriate for difficulty level
   - Ensure scenarios balance technical challenges with banking regulations

2. **Information Control**
   - Determine what information is available to participants at each stage
   - Prepare answers for likely questions from participants
   - Create physical or digital information cards for scenarios

3. **Environment Setup**
   - Arrange the exercise space to simulate banking operations
   - Prepare relevant displays and monitoring systems
   - Consider appropriate materials for a banking environment

### During Exercise Facilitation

1. **Maintaining Banking Atmosphere**
   - Introduce complications related to the high-stakes banking environment
   - Provide realistic time pressures with regulatory considerations
   - Balance technical challenges with client confidentiality concerns

2. **Adaptability**
   - Be prepared to adjust scenario pacing based on participant progress
   - Have additional syndicate activities ready if teams resolve issues quickly
   - Be willing to provide hints if teams get completely stuck

3. **Observation**
   - Take notes on key decisions and actions
   - Identify teaching moments for the debrief
   - Document specific areas for improvement

### Post-Exercise Activities

1. **Facilitating Discussion**
   - Use open-ended questions to promote reflection
   - Focus on process improvements rather than assigning blame
   - Highlight both strengths and areas for improvement

2. **Documentation**
   - Compile observations and participant feedback
   - Prepare a comprehensive after-action report
   - Develop specific, actionable recommendations

- Client Portal Activity:
  - Affected Accounts: 17 high-net-worth clients with portfolios exceeding €10M
  - Login Times: Between 1:30 AM and 4:45 AM Zurich time
  - Activities: Viewing portfolio details, investment history, personal information
  - Access Method: Valid credentials with correct multi-factor authentication

### Technical Details for Scenario 3
- Authentication Bypass Attempts:
  ```
  Method: Manipulation of SMS verification timing
  Pattern: Systematic testing of timeout parameters
  Accounts Targeted: Primarily wealth management clients
  Success Rate: 4 successful bypasses out of 12 attempts
  Technique: Exploiting race condition in authentication sequence
  ```

### Technical Details for Scenario 5
- Malware Characteristics:
  - Type: Custom-built financial transaction interceptor
  - Capabilities: SWIFT message monitoring and selective modification
  - Installation: Executed through compromised admin credentials
  - Command & Control: Encrypted communication through HTTPS on standard ports
  - Evasion Techniques: Dormant except during specific transaction conditions
  - Target: International wire transfers exceeding €500,000

### Technical Details for Scenario 7
- Payment Anomalies:
  - Affected Transfers: 7 transactions totaling €12.7 million
  - Destination Structure: Initially to legitimate correspondent banks, then through multiple hops
  - Authorization: All transfers have proper digital signatures and approvals
  - Timing: Initiated to align with end-of-day processing to maximize clearing time
  - Pattern: Each transfer modified to include routing instructions in reference fields
  - Final Destinations: Analysis suggests funds ultimately routing to banks in Singapore and UAE

### Technical Details for Scenario 9

  Known Members Identified:

### Technical Details for Scenario 10

  1. Initial access through targeted phishing of relationship managers
