# Deep Space Command: Stellar Breach
## A Space Exploration-Themed Cybersecurity Tabletop Exercise

## Exercise Overview

**Title:** Deep Space Command: Stellar Breach  
**Duration:** 4 hours (recommended)  
**Target Audience:** Mission Controllers, Astrotech Specialists, Orbital Security Officers, Command Staff  
**Difficulty:** Specialist to Commander-level  
**Objective:** Test the Interplanetary Space Agency's ability to detect, contain, and neutralize a sophisticated cyberattack targeting critical mission systems aboard the Artemis Deep Space Station and connected orbital facilities.

## Learning Objectives

1. Evaluate team coordination during complex security incidents in space environments
2. Test response capabilities with communication delays and limited resources
3. Assess communication protocols between Earth control and orbital facilities
4. Practice decision-making under pressure with degraded communications
5. Identify gaps in current space-based security measures

## Exercise Structure

### Preparation Phase (2 weeks prior)

1. **Mission Director Selection:** Appoint 1-2 individuals to coordinate the exercise
2. **Crew Selection:** Identify key personnel from various mission divisions
3. **Resource Preparation:** Ready the necessary documentation, communication protocols, and simulated mission control environment
4. **Pre-Exercise Briefing:** Conduct a mission briefing explaining exercise parameters and expectations

### Exercise Roles

1. **Mission Director:** Controls exercise flow, introduces scenarios, evaluates responses
2. **Mission Controllers:** Personnel responsible for monitoring spacecraft systems
3. **Astrotech Specialists:** Technical specialists who respond to security incidents
4. **Orbital Security Officers:** Security specialists focused on threat containment
5. **Command Staff:** Decision-makers who balance mission objectives with security
6. **Observers:** Record actions, decisions, and potential improvements
7. **Partner Agency Representatives:** (Optional) Add realism with international space agency coordination

## Exercise Materials

### Required Documentation

1. Space station security protocols

## Scenario Background

The Interplanetary Space Agency (ISA) operates the Artemis Deep Space Station, a cutting-edge research and exploration platform in cislunar orbit, along with multiple smaller research facilities throughout the solar system. ISA provides mission control and security monitoring for:

- Artemis Deep Space Station (primary habitat and research)
- Lunar Gateway (transfer station and communications hub)
- Mars Forward Base (advance research outpost)
- Europa Explorer (deep space probe with sensitive instruments)

The agency utilizes a sophisticated Mission Control Network that receives telemetry and security data from all space assets, with a centralized command center for monitoring and response.

## Exercise Narrative

An advanced threat actor with suspected nation-state backing, known as "Cosmic Silence," has identified ISA's Mission Control Network as a prime target for infiltration. Their objective is to compromise critical life support and navigation systems to either steal proprietary research data or potentially sabotage international space missions. The attack will unfold in multiple stages over the course of the exercise, combining both technical exploits and social engineering of ground and space personnel.

## Exercise Timeline and Scenarios

### Phase 1: Initial Detection (0:00-1:00)

**Setting the Scene (0:00-0:10)**
- Mission Director introduces the scenario as a routine day at ISA Mission Control
- Teams are at their stations monitoring nominal operations of all space assets

**Scenario 1 (0:10): Unusual Telemetry Patterns**
- A mission controller notices unusual fluctuations in life support telemetry from Artemis Station
- The data patterns don't trigger emergency alerts but show subtle deviations from expected parameters
- The anomalies appear during scheduled communication windows with Earth

*Expected Actions:*
- Investigate the telemetry anomalies
- Cross-check with historical data patterns
- Verify sensor integrity through diagnostic routines
- Begin documenting the incident in the mission log

**Scenario 2 (0:30): Unauthorized Command Sequence**
- The Europa Explorer probe executes an unscheduled course correction burn
- The command appears to have originated from an authorized ground station
- The correction is minor but diverts the probe from its planned observation window

*Expected Actions:*
- Immediately contact probe control team
- Verify command authorization chain
- Consider reverting to backup navigation parameters
- Begin investigating command origin and authentication

**Scenario 3 (0:45): Unusual Network Activity**
- Network monitoring detects unusual data transfers from the scientific database to the communication array
- The transfers use valid credentials from a mission specialist currently on EVA
- Preliminary analysis suggests research data is being staged for external transmission

*Expected Actions:*
- Activate security incident response procedures
- Assign investigation teams with specific roles
- Consider temporarily restricting certain data access
- Initiate enhanced monitoring of communication systems

### Phase 2: Escalation (1:00-2:00)

**Scenario 4 (1:00): Partner Agency Alert**
- The European Space Agency reports unusual connection attempts to shared systems
- The attempts appear to originate from ISA's lunar communications relay
- The connection attempts target joint mission planning databases

*Expected Actions:*
- Acknowledge the potential connection to earlier anomalies
- Collaborate with ESA security team
- Investigate lunar relay systems for compromise
- Update incident documentation and escalate internally

**Scenario 5 (1:20): Advanced Persistent Threat Detection**
- Deep analysis of Artemis Station systems reveals a sophisticated, previously undetected malware
- The malware appears designed to manipulate environmental control systems
- Evidence indicates it has been dormant for approximately 45 days, possibly installed during the last supply mission

*Expected Actions:*
- Perform detailed code analysis despite bandwidth limitations
- Begin investigating initial infection vector
- Consider implications for crew safety and station integrity
- Update incident response team and command staff

**Scenario 6 (1:40): Evidence Tampering**
- Security logs from critical periods begin showing signs of manipulation
- Backup system logs remain intact but show discrepancies with primary records
- Attempts to modify automated safety protocols are detected

*Expected Actions:*
- Preserve evidence before further tampering occurs
- Implement additional monitoring of critical systems
- Consider isolation procedures for affected systems
- Update risk assessment based on potential safety impacts

### Phase 3: Crisis Management (2:00-3:00)

**Scenario 7 (2:00): Life Support Anomalies**
- Artemis Station reports gradual, unexplained changes to oxygen mixture levels
- The changes are subtle enough to evade automatic safeguards
- Station crew reports mild symptoms consistent with atmospheric irregularities

*Expected Actions:*
- Prioritize crew safety with immediate countermeasures
- Implement manual overrides of life support systems
- Coordinate with medical team for crew assessment
- Prepare for potential emergency procedures

**Scenario 8 (2:20): Agency Director Involvement**
- ISA Director demands immediate briefing on the situation
- International partners express concern about joint mission impacts
- Media outlets begin reporting on "unusual activities" at mission control

*Expected Actions:*
- Prepare concise executive summary with verified facts
- Organize information on safety measures implemented
- Advise on public relations approach
- Continue technical response activities with minimal disruption

**Scenario 9 (2:40): Attacker Communication**
- A message appears on a mission control terminal claiming responsibility
- The attackers demand research data from the Europa mission and proprietary propulsion technologies
- They threaten to further compromise life support systems if demands aren't met

*Expected Actions:*
- Document the communication completely
- Assess authenticity and threat credibility
- Discuss response options with leadership
- Consider law enforcement and intelligence agency notifications
- Prepare for escalated containment measures

### Phase 4: Resolution and Recovery (3:00-4:00)

**Scenario 10 (3:00): Attack Vector Identified**
- Forensic analysis reveals the complete attack path and techniques used
- Evidence points to a sophisticated nation-state actor with space technology interests
- A compromised update package for navigation systems is identified as the initial entry point

*Expected Actions:*
- Document all findings for comprehensive analysis
- Develop a prioritized remediation plan considering space-based constraints
- Identify critical security gaps for immediate attention
- Prepare technical details for affected systems and partners

**Scenario 11 (3:20): Containment Decision Point**
- The incident response team must decide on final containment strategy
- Options include temporary disconnection of ground-station linkages vs. selective isolation
- Each option impacts different mission parameters and recovery timeframes

*Expected Actions:*
- Evaluate safety, mission, and security trade-offs
- Make decisions based on comprehensive risk assessment
- Communicate decisions and rationale to all stakeholders
- Begin implementing the selected approach

**Scenario 12 (3:40): Recovery Planning**
- With immediate threats contained, focus shifts to recovery and future missions
- Multiple systems require secure restoration and verification
- International partners and oversight committees require detailed incident reports

*Expected Actions:*
- Develop a prioritized recovery sequence beginning with life-critical systems
- Create a communication plan for different stakeholders
- Prepare preliminary incident notifications
- Begin documenting lessons learned for future missions

### Conclusion (3:50-4:00)

- Mission Director declares the end of the exercise
- Brief initial feedback from participants
- Schedule a formal debrief session for the following day

## Exercise Evaluation

### Evaluation Metrics

1. **Detection Effectiveness**
   - Time to detect initial anomalies
   - Ability to correlate related security events across space assets
   - Thoroughness of investigation given communication constraints

2. **Response Efficiency**
   - Time from detection to initial response
   - Appropriateness of response actions for space environments
   - Resource allocation and utilization with limited options

3. **Communication Effectiveness**
   - Ground-to-space communication clarity and efficiency
   - Partner agency coordination
   - Command updates and escalations

4. **Decision Quality**
   - Crew safety prioritization
   - Decision-making under communication delays
   - Balance between security and mission continuity

### Post-Exercise Activities

1. **Hot Wash (Immediately following exercise)**
   - Quick round-table discussion of initial impressions
   - Identification of major strengths and challenges
   - Collection of immediate feedback

2. **Formal Mission Review (1-2 days after exercise)**
   - Structured review of exercise timeline and decisions
   - Analysis of major decision points
   - Documentation of lessons learned

3. **Improvement Planning (1-2 weeks after exercise)**
   - Development of specific action items
   - Assignment of responsibilities for improvements
   - Timeline for implementing changes

4. **Follow-up Simulation (3-6 months later)**
   - Targeted scenario to test improvements
   - Focus on previously identified weaknesses
   - Validate effectiveness of changes

## Mission Director Guidelines

### Pre-Exercise Preparation

1. **Scenario Customization**
   - Adjust technical details to match your specific space infrastructure
   - Modify asset names and configurations as appropriate
   - Ensure scenarios are realistic for your mission profiles and technologies

2. **Information Control**
   - Determine what information is available to participants at each stage
   - Prepare answers for likely questions from participants
   - Create physical or digital information cards for scenarios

3. **Environment Setup**
   - Arrange the exercise space to simulate mission control
   - Prepare displays showing relevant telemetry
   - Implement communication delays where appropriate

### During Exercise Facilitation

1. **Maintaining Realism**
   - Introduce complications related to space environments (delays, limited bandwidth, etc.)
   - Provide realistic time pressures
   - Limit information as would happen in real space missions

2. **Adaptability**
   - Be prepared to adjust scenario pacing based on participant progress
   - Have additional scenarios ready if teams resolve issues quickly
   - Be willing to provide hints if teams get completely stuck

3. **Observation**
   - Take notes on key decisions and actions
   - Identify teaching moments for the debrief
   - Document specific areas for improvement

### Post-Exercise Activities

1. **Facilitating Discussion**
   - Use open-ended questions to promote reflection
   - Focus on process improvements rather than assigning blame
   - Highlight both strengths and areas for improvement

2. **Documentation**
   - Compile observations and participant feedback
   - Prepare a comprehensive after-action report
   - Develop specific, actionable recommendations

## Appendix: Detailed Technical Scenarios

### Technical Details for Scenario 1
- Affected Systems: Artemis Station Environmental Monitoring
- Anomaly Pattern: 2.3% fluctuation in CO2 levels outside normal diurnal variation
- Timestamp: Mission Day 234, 03:27 GMT
- Duration: Intermittent, appearing only during scheduled Earth communications
- Correlation: Fluctuations align precisely with ground station handovers

### Technical Details for Scenario 2
- Probe Function: Europa Explorer Deep Space Probe
- Command Type: Trajectory Adjustment Burn (TAB-17)
- Apparent Origin: Madrid Ground Station (validated credentials)
- Burn Parameters: 3.2 second burn, resulting in 0.4-degree course adjustment
- Authentication: Valid TAB signature using current mission keys

### Technical Details for Scenario 3
- Data Transfer Specifics:
  ```
  Source: /sci/europa/subsurface/raw/23.05.17/
  Destination: /comm/queue/priority/
  Volume: 1.7 TB (compressed)
  User: dr.zhang.eva
  Process: Non-standard transfer utility (masked as system process)
  ```
- User Status: Dr. Zhang confirmed on EVA during transfer time
- Data Classification: Level 4 (Restricted) scientific findings from Europa

### Technical Details for Scenario 5
- Malware Characteristics:
  - Custom space-hardened code with radiation-tolerant error handling
  - Uses legitimate system maintenance routines as cover
  - Specifically targets Environmental Control and Life Support Systems (ECLSS)
  - Designed to operate within normal sensor parameters while gradually altering setpoints
  - Command and control through encoded telemetry bursts:
    - Disguised as routine status updates
    - Uses spread-spectrum techniques to avoid detection
    - Contains encoded instructions for next phase activation

### Technical Details for Scenario 7
- Life Support Anomalies:
  - O2 percentage: Gradual decrease from 21.0% to 19.7% (below alarm thresholds)
  - N2 pressure: Corresponding increase to maintain total pressure
  - Temperature: 0.5°C increase in crew quarters
  - Timeline: Changes implemented over 72-hour period to avoid detection
  - System Response: All automatic diagnostics report within acceptable ranges

### Technical Details for Scenario 10
- Attack Path:
  1. Initial access via compromised software update package for navigation systems
  2. Lateral movement through shared infrastructure to life support systems
  3. Credential harvesting from local system caches
  4. Establishment of persistent access through modified firmware
  5. Defense evasion using log manipulation and sensor recalibration
  6. Command and control via encoded telemetry bursts
  7. Data exfiltration through scheduled high-bandwidth science transmissions
